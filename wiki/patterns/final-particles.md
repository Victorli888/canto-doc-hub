# Final Particles

The highest-leverage page in this wiki. Textbook Cantonese is largely real Cantonese with
the particles stripped out — grammatically fine, and instantly recognizable as a learner.
A particle is rarely optional in speech; dropping it doesn't make the sentence neutral, it
makes it blunt.

Particles stack, and the stacks carry their own meaning (喇喎, 囉喎, 添㗎). Get the single
particles solid first; the stacks come along on their own.

## Softening and suggesting

### 啦 — laa1
- **Jyutping:** laa1
- **Use:** Turns an order into a suggestion, or urges gently. 「走啦」(zau2 laa1) is "let's
  go" / "go on then", not "leave." 「食飯啦」= "let's eat."
- **Register:** everywhere in speech. Neutral.
- **Contrast:** bare 「走」is an order. The particle is what makes it an invitation. This
  is the single most common thing missing from textbook-sounding Cantonese.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-01 · source: seed
- **See also:** [[softening-and-politeness]]

### 吖 — aa1
- **Jyutping:** aa1
- **Use:** Softens a request into a favour. 「幫我手吖」(bong1 ngo5 sau2 aa1) = "give me a
  hand, would you?"
- **Register:** casual to neutral. Friendly.
- **Contrast:** 「幫我手」alone is a directive. With 吖 it's a request you can decline.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-01 · source: seed
- **See also:** [[softening-and-politeness]]

## Marking a change of state

### 喇 — laa3
- **Jyutping:** laa3
- **Use:** Something has changed — it's done, it's time, it's true *now*.
  「搞掂喇」= "it's done (now)." 「我走喇」= "I'm off."
- **Register:** everywhere.
- **Contrast:** often written 啦 in casual texting, but it is a different particle.
  laa1 suggests; laa3 reports a change. 「走啦」= "let's go"; 「走喇」= "I'm leaving."
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-01 · source: seed
- **See also:** [[work-small-talk]]

## Attitude

### 囉 — lo1 / lo3
- **Jyutping:** lo1 (stating the obvious) · lo3 (resignation, "as expected")
- **Use:** Like 喎, one character covering two particles. **lo1** marks something as
  self-evident — matter-of-fact, sometimes faintly sarcastic: 「係囉」= "yeah, obviously."
  **lo3** carries resignation or an expected change of state: 「冇辦法囉」(mou5 baan6 faat3
  lo3) = "nothing to be done, then."
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** bare 「係」is a flat yes. 係囉 is agreement *with an attitude* — the thing
  that makes a conversation sound like two people rather than a transcript. Note that
  agreement 係囉 is also commonly cited as hai6 lo3; the two overlap here and the
  distinction is softer than for 喎.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-01 · source: seed · verified against jyutping.org and
  Wiktionary
- **See also:** [[lazy-sounds]]

### 嘛 — maa3
- **Jyutping:** maa3
- **Use:** Offers a reason as self-evident. 「我唔識嘛」(ngo5 m4 sik1 maa3) = "well — I
  don't know how, do I."
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** stating the reason bare sounds defensive; 嘛 frames it as something the
  listener should already have known.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-01 · source: seed

### 添 — tim1
- **Jyutping:** tim1
- **Use:** "And on top of that." 「仲要落雨添」(zung6 jiu3 lok6 jyu5 tim1) = "and it's
  raining as well."
- **Register:** casual.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-01 · source: seed

## Source of information

### 喎 — wo3 / wo5
- **Jyutping:** wo3 (reminder, surprise) · wo5 (hearsay)
- **Use:** Two particles sharing a character, and the distinction is worth learning.
  **wo3** flags something noteworthy or contrary to what the listener assumed — a casual
  reminder or a realization: 「佢唔嚟喎」= "hey — he's not coming." **wo5** marks reported
  speech: 「佢話佢唔嚟喎」= "he said he's not coming (so I hear)."
- **Register:** casual to neutral.
- **Contrast:** English carries hearsay with a whole clause ("apparently", "I heard
  that"). Cantonese does it with one syllable. Overusing the clause is a tell.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-01 · source: seed · verified against Wiktionary and
  the EdUHK paper on sentence-final particles
- **See also:** [[work-small-talk]]

### 咩 — me1
- **Jyutping:** me1
- **Use:** Turns a statement into an incredulous question. 「係咩?」(hai6 me1) = "really?
  is that so?"
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** 「係唔係?」is a neutral yes/no question. 咩 carries doubt — you're not
  asking, you're pushing back.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-01 · source: seed

### 㗎 — gaa3 / gaa4
- **Jyutping:** gaa3 (stating a matter of fact) · gaa4 (asking for confirmation)
- **Literal:** a contraction — 嘅 + 啊 gives gaa3, 嘅 + 呀 gives gaa4 (Matthews & Yip).
- **Use:** **gaa3** presents the sentence as how things are, often correcting an
  assumption the other person just revealed: 「我寫code㗎」= "I write code, actually."
  「咁衰㗎!」= "that's awful!" **gaa4** turns it into a check: 「係唔係真㗎?」= "is that
  really true?", 「你當我傻㗎?」= "you think I'm stupid?"
- **Register:** casual to neutral, everywhere in speech.
- **Contrast:** the bare statement 「我寫code」just reports. 㗎 adds "in case you were
  picturing something else" — which is why it carries explanations and gentle
  corrections. Because it contains 嘅, it feels like 「我係寫code嘅」compressed.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-04 · source: [[describing-my-job-dump]] · verified
  against Wiktionary (Matthews & Yip 2011)
- **See also:** [[describing-my-job]], [[softening-and-politeness]]
