# Source — "Explaining Finances to Mom" sheet

Raw file: [`raw/2026-11-04-explaining-finances-to-mom.md`](../../raw/2026-11-04-explaining-finances-to-mom.md)

A sheet Victor brought in on 2026-11-04 for a specific conversation: telling his mother
that her money sits in two unconnected banks, that neither can see the other, and what a
high-yield savings account and a Roth IRA are. Four vocabulary tables, thirteen sentences
in conversation order, and a short dialogue with her likely questions.

Fanned out to [[talking-about-money]] and [[banking-and-finance]], with 呀嘛 added to
[[final-particles]].

Verified against the local dataset ([[words-hk-dataset]]) in about ninety seconds —
the first ingest took the better part of fifteen minutes for the same work.

## What it got right

Much more than the first sheet. The banking vocabulary is clean throughout: 儲蓄
cyu5 cuk1, 往來 wong5 loi4, 結餘 git3 jyu4, 結單 git3 daan1, 密碼 mat6 maa5, 免稅
min5 seoi3, 提款 tai4 fun2, 存款 cyun4 fun2, 賺 zaan6 (right character, right tone).

Two judgments in it are better than they look:

**戶口, not 帳戶.** Correct, and worth more than it seems — words.hk's own definitions
are written using 帳户, so the written-Chinese pull toward 帳戶 is real. 戶口 is the
spoken Hong Kong word.

**「冇唔見呀嘛?」** reads like a learner's double negative, and it is not. words.hk's
entry for 呀嘛 gives, as one of its three senses: *放喺句尾，用嚟確認最壞嘅情況有冇發生；
通常同否定句一齊用* — "used at the end of a sentence to confirm whether the worst-case
scenario has happened; usually together with a negative sentence." A worried mother asking
whether her money has gone missing is precisely that. The instinct was right.

## Orthography and register notes

Not errors, but worth recording so nobody "corrects" them later in the wrong direction:

- **解釋下 → 解釋吓.** The reading `haa5` is right. The character is the fiddly part:
  words.hk files the haa5 suffix under 下, but writes it 吓 in actual word entries
  (試吓 si3 haa5, 郁吓 juk1 haa5). The wiki already uses 吓 in 傾吓 and 睇吓, so 吓 it is.
  Do **not** trust a bare character lookup here — standalone 吓 is haa1/haa2, the
  interjection "huh?", a completely different word. See [[words-hk-dataset]].
- **咁 in 「好似…咁」.** The reading `gam2` is right for the analogy. Strictly, words.hk
  writes gam2 as 噉 and reserves 咁 for gam3 (the degree word, as in 得咁少 — which the
  sheet uses correctly). Everyday Hong Kong writing uses 咁 for both, which is what the
  sheet does, so this is a spelling convention rather than a mistake.
- **獨立** — rime lists `duk6 laap6` first and `duk6 lap6` second. The sheet's lap6 is
  attested, not wrong. Moot in practice: 完全冇關係 is the phrase to use with Mom, and
  獨立 is the one that would sound like a bank letter.
- **知道 in phrase 4.** 「一個唔會知道另一個有幾多錢」works, but 知道 is the fuller,
  slightly written form. 「一個唔知另一個有幾多錢」is tighter and more spoken, and in an
  analogy you want the short version. Judgment call, flagged on the entry.

## On the finance content

The sheet's own accuracy note is correct, and unusually careful: FDIC covers deposits
rather than investments held inside a Roth IRA, which is why phrase 11 says 銀行啲錢;
and contributions come out freely while earnings are what attract a penalty, which is why
phrase 10 says 賺返嚟嘅錢.

One thing it leaves out: tax-free withdrawal of Roth **earnings** needs the account to
have been open five years as well as the age condition. 未夠年紀 covers only the age half.
This changes no Cantonese — 可能要罰錢 stays true either way — but if Mom asks "how long",
the age is not the whole answer.

## Still open

- 高息儲蓄戶口 is a descriptive compound, not a Hong Kong product name — US high-yield
  savings accounts have no HK equivalent to borrow a name from. Every component is
  verified and the compound is transparent, but it is assembled rather than attested.
- 往來戶口, 轉賬, 冇關係, 高息 and 賺返 are all absent from the rime tables. Each
  component checks out, so these are gaps in the data rather than doubts about the words.
