# Source — feedback on the everyday-money line

Raw file: [`raw/2026-11-12-everyday-money-phrasing-feedback.md`](../../raw/2026-11-12-everyday-money-phrasing-feedback.md)

Victor pasted this on 2026-11-12. It's a critique of one line in the Wells Fargo
breakdown, 「仲有五千蚊擺喺 checking account，係你日常用嗰筆」, which the wiki had composed
on 2026-11-09. Its author isn't stated. It agrees the line is grammatical but says it
sounds translated. It offers three replacements and rewrites the whole Wells Fargo
sentence.

Fanned out to [[talking-about-money]] (the 擺咗做定期 and 畀你平時用嘅 blocks) and
[[banking-and-finance]].

## Takeaways

- **平時用 / 平時使, not 日常用.** 日常 is real HK Cantonese, but it lives in
  set phrases like 日常生活 and 日常開支. For "the money you spend day to day", 平時 is
  the spoken word. words.hk agrees: it defines 日常 *as* 平時嘅.
- **嗰筆 was not the problem.** 嗰筆錢 is ordinary. The stiffness comes from 日常用
  and 嗰筆 together. This corrects the wiki's first diagnosis, which blamed 嗰筆 for "a
  bookkeeping feel".
- **擺咗做定期, not 擺咗落定期存款.** The short noun 定期 plus 做 is how people
  say "put it in a fixed deposit". Confirmed in Hong Kong personal-finance writing:
  MoneyHero runs articles headed 借錢做定期. The local dataset only has the adverb sense
  of 定期 ("regularly"), so this one was checked on the web.
- **未到期就攞唔到出嚟** replaces 鎖住咗，到期先攞得出嚟 as the lead version. Same
  meaning, said from the "you can't" side.

## Accuracy note

The source itself flags this, and it's correct: a CD or fixed deposit *can* usually be
broken early, at the cost of a penalty, normally lost interest. US CDs work the same way.
So 攞唔到 is a simplification. It's fine as the first sentence to Mom, but if she asks
"what if I need it?", the honest answer is "yes, but you lose interest". The wiki doesn't
have a verified Cantonese line for that yet.
