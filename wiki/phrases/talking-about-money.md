# Talking About Money

Explaining money to family — which in practice means explaining it to a parent who is
worried, where the hard part is not the vocabulary but keeping the register warm and the
sentences short. Terms are in [[banking-and-finance]].

The organising insight from the first sheet on this: **the analogy does the work the
explanation can't.** 「好似兩個銀包咁」gets further in six syllables than any amount of
correct banking vocabulary, because the problem was never that Mom lacked the word for
"balance".

## Explaining and reassuring

### 好似…咁 — it's like …
- **Jyutping:** hou2 ci5 … gam2
- **Moment:** explaining to Mom that two banks can't see each other's money
- **Use:** The analogy frame, and the most useful thing on this page. Sets up a comparison
  to something concrete: 「好似兩個銀包咁」= "it's like two wallets."
- **Register:** casual to neutral, everywhere. Especially good with elders — it replaces a
  technical explanation with a picture.
- **Contrast:** strictly, words.hk writes this gam2 as 噉 and keeps 咁 for gam3 (the degree
  word — 咁多, 得咁少). Everyday Hong Kong writing uses 咁 for both, so 咁 here is fine;
  just don't read it as gam3.
- **Example:** 好似兩個銀包咁，一個唔知另一個有幾多錢。— hou2 ci5 loeng5 go3 ngan4 baau1
  gam2, jat1 go3 m4 zi1 ling6 jat1 go3 jau5 gei2 do1 cin2. "It's like two wallets — one
  doesn't know how much is in the other." (The sheet had 唔會知道; 唔知 is tighter, and in
  an analogy you want the short version.)
- **Status:** learning · captured 2026-11-04 · practised 2026-11-11 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]
- **See also:** [[banking-and-finance]]

### 完全冇關係 — nothing to do with each other
- **Jyutping:** jyun4 cyun4 mou5 gwaan1 hai6
- **Moment:** telling Mom the two banks are unconnected
- **Use:** Two things are unrelated. 「呢兩間銀行完全冇關係㗎」. Carries 㗎 well, because
  this is usually said to correct an assumption.
- **Register:** casual to neutral.
- **Contrast:** 獨立 (duk6 laap6) is the word a bank letter would use — "independent".
  Correct, and it makes you sound like the letter. 冇關係 is what you say to your mother.
  Note 冇關係 also means "it doesn't matter" in reply to an apology; context separates them
  completely and nobody is confused in practice.
- **Status:** learning · captured 2026-11-04 · practised 2026-11-11 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]
- **See also:** [[final-particles]], [[banking-and-finance]]

### 睇唔到 — can't see it
- **Jyutping:** tai2 m4 dou2
- **Moment:** why one bank's app doesn't show the other bank's balance
- **Use:** The verb + 唔到 pattern: unable to, despite trying. 「你喺呢間睇唔到嗰間嘅結餘」.
- **Register:** casual, universal.
- **Contrast:** 唔睇 (m4 tai2) is "doesn't look" — a choice. 睇唔到 is "can't see" — an
  inability. For a worried parent that difference is the whole reassurance: the bank isn't
  hiding it, it just can't reach it.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-04 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]

### 唔使擔心 — don't worry
- **Jyutping:** m4 sai2 daam1 sam1
- **Use:** Closes a reassurance. 「銀行啲錢有政府保障，唔使擔心」.
- **Register:** warm, casual to neutral.
- **Contrast:** 唔好擔心 (m4 hou2 …) is closer to "don't you worry" — a mild instruction.
  唔使 is "there's no need", which gives a reason rather than an order, and lands better
  with a parent. Same 唔使 / 唔好 split as in [[softening-and-politeness]].
- **Status:** learning · captured 2026-11-04 · practised 2026-11-11 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]
- **See also:** [[softening-and-politeness]]

## Money moving around

### 分開擺 — kept separately
- **Jyutping:** fan1 hoi1 baai2
- **Moment:** her money is in two places, and that's deliberate, not an accident
- **Use:** 「你啲錢分開咗擺喺兩間銀行」= "your money is split across two banks."
  擺 (baai2) is the everyday "put / keep somewhere".
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** the measure word follows the noun, not the money: 兩**間**銀行 but
  兩**個**戶口. Swapping 銀行 for 戶口 and keeping 間 is the slip to avoid.
- **Example:** 冇唔見，全部都喺度，只係分開咗擺。— mou5 m4 gin3, cyun4 bou6 dou1 hai2
  dou6, zi2 hai6 fan1 hoi1 zo2 baai2. "Nothing's lost, it's all there, just kept in
  separate places."
- **Status:** learning · captured 2026-11-04 · practised 2026-11-11 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]
- **See also:** [[banking-and-finance]]

### 隨時都可以 — you can … anytime
- **Jyutping:** ceoi4 si4 dou1 ho2 ji5
- **Use:** The reassurance that money isn't locked up. 「啲錢隨時都可以攞出嚟」.
  The 都 is not optional — 隨時可以 alone sounds clipped.
- **Register:** casual to neutral.
- **Status:** learning · captured 2026-11-04 · practised 2026-11-11 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]

### 幫你轉賬 — I'll do the transfer for you
- **Jyutping:** bong1 nei5 zyun2 zoeng3
- **Use:** 「如果要轉錢，我可以幫你轉賬」. Note the register split inside one sentence:
  轉錢 (zyun2 cin2) for what she wants to do, 轉賬 (zyun2 zoeng3) for the banking
  operation. That's natural, not inconsistent.
- **Register:** 轉錢 casual, 轉賬 neutral/banking.
- **Status:** learning · captured 2026-11-04 · practised 2026-11-11 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]
- **See also:** [[softening-and-politeness]] (幫我… is the same frame pointed the other way)

## Laying out the full picture

### 擺咗做定期 — put into a CD / fixed deposit
- **Jyutping:** baai2 zo2 zou6 ding6 kei4
- **Moment:** explaining that $20,000 at Wells Fargo is in a CD, separate from her $5,000 checking money
- **Use:** 「有兩萬蚊擺咗做定期」= "$20,000 is in a fixed deposit." 定期 on its own is the
  spoken short form of 定期存款, and 做定期 is how people say "put money in a fixed deposit".
  Follow it with 未到期就攞唔到出嚟 to explain why she can't just withdraw it.
- **Register:** casual, everyday.
- **Contrast:** the first version filed here was 擺咗落定期存款. That's correct, but it uses
  the full noun from the bank's product page. Say 做定期 out loud and 定期存款 on the form.
  The "locked" half has two shapes:
  - 未到期就攞唔到出嚟 (mei6 dou3 kei4 zau6 lo2 m4 dou2 ceot1 lai4): "can't take it out
    before it matures". This is the lead version.
  - 鎖住咗，到期先攞得出嚟 (so2 zyu6 zo2, dou3 kei4 sin1 lo2 dak1 ceot1 lai4): "it's locked,
    only out once it matures". Same meaning, said from the "you can" side.
  Either way it gives the reason, not just the rule, same as 唔使擔心 above. **Accuracy:**
  a CD can usually be broken early if you give up interest, so 攞唔到 is a simplification.
  See [[everyday-money-phrasing-feedback]].
- **Example:** Wells Fargo 嗰邊，有兩萬蚊擺咗做定期，未到期就攞唔到出嚟。另外仲有五千蚊喺
  checking account，畀你平時用嘅。— Wells Fargo go2 bin1, jau5 loeng5 maan6 man1 baai2 zo2
  zou6 ding6 kei4, mei6 dou3 kei4 zau6 lo2 m4 dou2 ceot1 lai4. ling6 ngoi6 zung6 jau5 ng5
  cin1 man1 hai2 checking account, bei2 nei5 ping4 si4 jung6 ge3. "At Wells Fargo, $20,000
  is in a fixed deposit; you can't take it out before it matures. On top of that there's
  $5,000 in checking, for your everyday use."
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-09 · practised 2026-11-11 · at home, walking Mom through her accounts · revised 2026-11-12 · source: [[everyday-money-phrasing-feedback]]
- **See also:** [[banking-and-finance]], [[talking-about-money#畀你平時用嘅]]

### 畀你平時用嘅 — it's for your everyday spending
- **Jyutping:** bei2 nei5 ping4 si4 jung6 ge3
- **Moment:** telling Mom which pot is the everyday money: the $5,000 in checking, as
  opposed to the CD and the investments
- **Use:** Labels one pile of money as the spending money. Three shapes, all natural:
  - 「嗰五千蚊係畀你平時用嘅。」 go2 ng5 cin1 man1 hai6 bei2 nei5 ping4 si4 jung6 ge3.
    — "That $5,000 is for your everyday use." The plainest; 係…嘅 states it as a fact
    about that money.
  - 「仲有五千蚊喺 checking account，畀你平時使嘅。」 zung6 jau5 ng5 cin1 man1 hai2
    checking account, bei2 nei5 ping4 si4 sai2 ge3. — "Another $5,000 is in checking,
    for you to spend." The most conversational. 使 (sai2) is specifically "spend", as in
    使錢, and is sharper than 用.
  - 「仲有五千蚊擺喺 checking account，方便你平時用。」 zung6 jau5 ng5 cin1 man1 baai2
    hai2 checking account, fong1 bin6 nei5 ping4 si4 jung6. — "Another $5,000 is in
    checking so you can use it whenever." Gives the reason (convenience). It's the same
    reassuring move as 唔使擔心: a reason, not a rule.
- **Register:** casual and warm. Right for a parent.
- **Contrast:** the version this page had before was 「係你日常用嗰筆」, which I composed on
  2026-11-09, and it is the stiff one. 日常 (jat6 soeng4) is a 區別詞, a noun modifier (日常
  生活, 日常開支) that reads like a form. words.hk defines it *with* 平時嘅, so 平時 is the
  spoken word underneath it. 嗰筆 on its own is fine (嗰筆錢 is everyday HK). What
  sounds translated is 日常用 and 嗰筆 together. The general rule:
  **平時 in speech, 日常 on paper.**
- **Note on 畀 / 俾:** both are bei2 "give / for / let". 畀 is the words.hk character; 俾 is
  the common popular spelling (as in 唔好俾人知 below). Same word.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-12 · replacing my 日常 version · source: [[everyday-money-phrasing-feedback]]
- **See also:** [[talking-about-money#擺咗做定期]], [[banking-and-finance]]

### 跟住股市升跌 — moves with the stock market
- **Jyutping:** gan1 zyu6 gu2 si5 sing1 dit3
- **Moment:** explaining that the $40,000 in ETFs, unlike the CD or the high-yield savings,
  isn't a fixed number
- **Use:** The honest framing for anything invested in funds — sets the expectation that
  the balance moves, which is the thing that worries a parent if it isn't said up front.
  「呢四萬蚊擺咗落 ETF 基金，跟住股市升跌」.
- **Register:** casual to neutral.
- **Contrast:** don't reach for 波動 (bo1 dung6, "volatility") — that's the word on the fund
  prospectus, not the one you say out loud to Mom.
- **Example:** 嗰度十四萬蚊擺喺高息儲蓄戶口，隨時都可以攞；四萬蚊喺 Roth IRA，即係退休戶口，
  通常留到退休先用；仲有四萬蚊擺咗落 ETF 基金，喺個人投資戶口度，跟住股市升跌。— "There,
  $140,000 is in the high-yield savings account, she can take it out anytime; $40,000 is in
  a Roth IRA — a retirement account, usually kept until retirement; and $40,000 more is in
  ETF funds, in the personal investment account, which moves with the stock market."
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-09 · practised 2026-11-11 · at home, walking Mom through her accounts
- **See also:** [[banking-and-finance]], [[final-particles]]

## Her side of the conversation

### 得咁少 — only this little
- **Jyutping:** dak1 gam3 siu2
- **Moment:** Mom looking at one bank's balance and thinking money has gone
- **Use:** 得 before a quantity means "only that much", with a note of disappointment.
  「點解呢間銀行話我得咁少錢嘅?」= "why does this bank say I've only got this little?"
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** this 咁 *is* gam3 — degree, "so/such" — unlike the gam2 of 好似…咁 above.
  Same character, two words. The sentence-final 嘅 here is ge2, the changed tone that makes
  it a genuine puzzled question rather than a statement.
- **Status:** learning · captured 2026-11-04 · practised 2026-11-11 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]
- **See also:** [[final-particles]], [[tone-sandhi]]

### 唔好俾人知 — don't let anyone know
- **Jyutping:** m4 hou2 bei2 jan4 zi1
- **Use:** 「唔好俾人知你個密碼呀」. 俾人知 is literally "give people to know" — the
  ordinary way to say "let someone find out".
- **Register:** casual. The 呀 (aa3) at the end is what keeps it caring rather than
  scolding; without it this is a telling-off.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-04 · source: [[explaining-finances-to-mom-dump]]
- **See also:** [[final-particles]]
