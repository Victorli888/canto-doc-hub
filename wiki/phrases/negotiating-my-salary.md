# Negotiating My Salary

Telling family how a job offer got negotiated up: what the offer was, what you asked
for, what they said back, where it landed. The listener is a parent, so the register is
warm and plain, not HR-speak. The good news is that Hong Kong already talks about this
half in English. **offer**, **bonus**, **budget**, **HR** and **percent** are all
ordinary Cantonese here, and the Chinese words around them are market words (開價,
還價, 傾), not contract words.

The job itself is [[describing-my-job]]. Money talk with Mom is [[talking-about-money]].

## The offer

### 收到offer — got an offer
- **Jyutping:** sau1 dou2 o1 faa4
- **Moment:** telling my parents I got an offer letter for $100,000 after the interviews
- **Use:** *offer* is the normal word for a job offer, measure word 個. words.hk's example
  is 「間公司畀咗個offer我」(gaan1 gung1 si1 bei2 zo2 go3 o1 faa4 ngo5), "the company gave
  me an offer". 收到offer and 畀咗個offer我 are the two ways to say it, from your side or
  theirs.
- **Register:** universal. Parents will know the word.
- **Contrast:** 聘用通知 (words.hk's own gloss) or 錄取 sound like a letter from a
  government department. Nobody says them about their own job.
- **Example:** 面試完之後，間公司畀咗個offer我，一年人工十萬美金。"After the interviews
  the company gave me an offer: a hundred thousand US a year." ⚠️ The sentence is
  composed. 畀咗個offer我, 人工 and 美金 are each verified.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[negotiating-my-salary#傾人工]]

### 人工 / 底薪 — salary / base salary
- **Jyutping:** jan4 gung1 / dai2 san1
- **Moment:** the $100,000 base on the offer letter
- **Use:** 人工 is the everyday word for pay: 你一個月幾錢人工啊? (words.hk). 底薪 is the
  specific part you're guaranteed, without the bonus. words.hk's example is 「做AO嘅底薪係
  四萬幾」. Use 底薪 when the base/bonus split matters, which it does here.
- **Register:** both are everyday speech.
- **Contrast:** 薪金 and 薪酬 are written words. 年薪 (nin4 san1) is fine but sounds like
  a job ad. 一年人工十萬 is what people actually say.
- **Numbers:** 十萬 (sap6 maan6) = 100,000. 十一萬 = 110,000. 十二萬 = 120,000.
  十一萬五 (sap6 jat1 maan6 ng5) = 115,000: a digit after 萬 means the next place down,
  the same way 一萬五 is 15,000.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents
- **See also:** [[banking-and-finance]]

### bonus / 花紅 — bonus
- **Jyutping:** bung1 nas4 / faa1 hung4
- **Moment:** the 8% bonus, based on personal and company performance
- **Use:** words.hk's definition of 花紅 already says it depends on 「公司業績同員工嘅表現」,
  which is exactly this bonus. *bonus* is what people say at work. 花紅 is the
  word an older parent may use. 8% is 八個percent (baat3 go3 pat6 sen1), the HK way, or
  百分之八 (baak3 fan6 zi1 baat3), which is more formal.
- **Register:** *bonus* is casual and at work. 花紅 is slightly older but fine with parents.
- **Contrast:** 獎金 (zoeng2 gam1) is the prize-money word, as in a competition.
- **Example:** 另外有八個percent bonus，睇我自己同公司嘅表現。"There's also an 8% bonus,
  depending on how I do and how the company does." ⚠️ The sentence is composed.
  八個percent, bonus and 表現 are each verified.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents · ⚠️ unverified

## The negotiation

### 傾人工 — negotiate salary
- **Jyutping:** king1 jan4 gung1
- **Moment:** explaining that I negotiated the offer instead of just accepting it
- **Use:** 傾 means both "chat" and "negotiate" (words.hk lists both senses). 傾人工 is
  the standard phrase: JobsDB HK titles its articles 「收Offer傾人工」. It also works
  without the noun: 「我同佢哋傾咗一輪」"I went back and forth with them a bit".
- **Register:** universal.
- **Contrast:** 談判 and 議價 are written or news words. Around it sit the market verbs:
  **壓價** (aat3 gaa3), when they push the number down; **還價** (waan4 gaa3), a counter-offer;
  **企硬** (kei5 ngaang6), holding firm. 講數 (gong2 sou3) also means negotiate, but it's
  slang and words.hk's example is a triad boss. That's funny with friends, not right for
  this story to parents.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents
- **See also:** [[work-small-talk#傾吓]]

### 專登開高啲 — deliberately ask high
- **Jyutping:** zyun1 dang1 hoi1 gou1 di1
- **Moment:** I asked for about $120,000, given 7 years' experience and Reno's cost of
  living, and used it as an anchor
- **Use:** 開價 (hoi1 gaa3) is "name the opening price", the seller's first number in a
  bargain. 開個價 is how you say it with a measure word. 專登 is "on purpose". "Anchoring"
  has no common Cantonese word, so describe what you did: you named a high number on
  purpose so there was room to come down. The follow-up 咁先有得傾 ("that way there's room
  to negotiate") is ⚠️ unverified as a set phrase, though 有得 + verb is ordinary.
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** 故意 (gu3 ji3) also means "deliberately", but words.hk says it carries a
  negative connotation, so it is closer to "out of spite". 專登 is the neutral one. Saying
  *anchor* in English to parents won't land.
- **Example:** 我有七年經驗，加上Reno嘅生活成本，我覺得底薪應該有十二萬左右，所以我專登開高啲。
  "With seven years' experience plus the cost of living in Reno, I figured the base
  should be around 120k, so I deliberately asked high." ⚠️ The sentence is composed.
  經驗 ging1 jim6, 左右 zo2 jau2 and 專登 are each verified. Victor corrected 生活指數 to
  生活成本 on 2026-11-20; see the next block.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[negotiating-my-salary#傾人工]], [[negotiating-my-salary#生活成本]]

### 生活成本 / 生活費 — cost of living / living expenses
- **Jyutping:** sang1 wut6 sing4 bun2 / sang1 wut6 fai3
- **Moment:** explaining why I needed a higher base: Reno's cost of living and what it
  costs me to live there
- **Use:** Two words for two jobs.
  - **生活成本** is the *level* of a place, "cost of living": Reno嘅生活成本高.
  - **生活費** is *your own* spending, "living expenses", the money that goes out each
    month: 我每個月嘅生活費. words.hk lists it as 維持生活嘅使費.
  When you're arguing for a number, the place justifies it (生活成本) and your bills make
  it concrete (生活費).
- **Register:** neutral. Both work with parents and at work.
- **Contrast:** I first used 生活指數 (sang1 wut6 zi2 sou3). words.hk does list it, and
  its own example (退休之後去一個生活指數低啲地方) reads well. But it's literally "cost-of-
  living *index*", which sounds like economics. Victor flagged 生活成本 as the better word,
  and it is the plain one.
- **Example:** Reno嘅生活成本唔低，我每個月嘅生活費都唔少，所以我覺得底薪要高啲。"Reno's
  cost of living isn't low, and my monthly living expenses add up, so I figured the base
  needed to be higher." ⚠️ The sentence is composed. 生活費 and 成本 sing4 bun2 are verified
  in words.hk. 生活成本 has no rime reading as a whole word, so the Jyutping is assembled
  from 生活 sang1 wut6 and 成本 sing4 bun2.
- **Status:** new · captured 2026-11-20 · from Victor, correcting 生活指數 · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[negotiating-my-salary#專登開高啲]], [[talking-about-money]]

### 太接近… — too close to …
- **Jyutping:** taai3 zip3 gan6
- **Moment:** they said 120k was too close to the L3 band for an L2 role
- **Use:** 接近 zip3 gan6 means "close to", in space or in amount. For "the L3 band", say
  L3嗰級嘅人工 ("what that level gets") rather than *band*. In words.hk, *band* only
  means the music kind, so salary-*band* is ⚠️ unverified as HK usage.
- **Register:** neutral.
- **Example:** 佢哋話十二萬太接近L3嗰級嘅人工，但我呢份係L2。"They said 120k was too
  close to L3 pay, and this job is L2." ⚠️ The sentence is composed.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents · ⚠️ unverified

### 最多得… — only … at most
- **Jyutping:** zeoi3 do1 dak1
- **Moment:** HR's budget for the role maxed out at 110,000
- **Use:** 最多 (zeoi3 do1) means "at most, the ceiling". 得 + amount is "only". Together
  they put a hard cap on a number. *budget* is ordinary Cantonese: words.hk lists it, and
  also the transliteration 筆直 (bat1 zik4).
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** 上限 (soeng6 haan6) and 預算 (jyu6 syun3) are correct but sound like the
  HR email. 最多得 is how you retell it.
- **Example:** HR嘅budget最多得十一萬。"HR's budget only went up to 110k." ⚠️ The
  sentence is composed. 最多, 得 and budget are each verified.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[talking-about-money#得咁少]]

### 要幾多先肯… — how much would it take for you to …
- **Jyutping:** jiu3 gei2 do1 sin1 hang2
- **Moment:** the hiring manager curtly asked what number I'd need
- **Use:** It means "need how much before you'll …". 先 here means "only then", and 肯
  means "be willing". It is the same 先肯 as in 「佢先肯食」on [[everyday-life]]. For the
  tone, 好直接噉問 ("asked straight out") is neutral. 好唔耐煩噉問 (m4 noi6 faan4) is
  "asked impatiently", if it really was curt.
- **Register:** casual and blunt, which suits a curt question.
- **Example:** 個hiring manager好直接噉問我：「你要幾多先肯簽？」"The hiring manager
  asked me straight out: 'What number do you need to sign?'" ⚠️ The sentence is
  composed. 幾多, 先, 肯 and 簽 are each verified.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[everyday-life#唔肯食]]

### 即刻簽 — sign right away
- **Jyutping:** zik1 hak1 cim1
- **Moment:** I said that if they got me to 115,000 I'd sign immediately
- **Use:** 即刻 (zik1 hak1) means "right now", and 簽 (cim1) means sign, including a
  contract. 如果有X，我即刻簽 is the closing move. A parent's natural reaction to it is
  爽快 (song2 faai3, "decisive, no dithering").
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** 立即 and 馬上 are fine, but 即刻 is the spoken default.
- **Example:** 我話：「如果有十一萬五，我即刻簽。」"I said, 'If it's 115k, I'll sign right
  now.'" ⚠️ The sentence is composed.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents · ⚠️ unverified

### 由…傾到… — negotiated from … up to …
- **Jyutping:** jau4 … king1 dou3 …
- **Moment:** the punchline: from 100,000 to 115,000, same 8% bonus
- **Use:** 由 X 傾到 Y (jau4 … king1 dou3 …) means "talked it from X to Y". The verb +
  到 marks where it ended up. For "the same", 冇變 ("unchanged") or 照舊 work.
- **Register:** casual.
- **Example:** 結果我由十萬傾到十一萬五，bonus都係八個percent，冇變。"In the end I talked
  it up from 100k to 115k, and the bonus stayed at 8%." ⚠️ The sentence is composed.
  冇變 is not in the local tables.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-19 · practised 2026-11-19 · explaining the salary negotiation to my parents · ⚠️ unverified

## The whole story in one go

面試完之後，間公司畀咗個offer我，底薪一年十萬美金，另外有八個percent bonus，睇我自己同公司嘅表現。
我有七年經驗，加上Reno嘅生活成本，我覺得底薪應該有十二萬左右，所以我專登開高啲。
佢哋話十二萬太接近L3嗰級嘅人工，但我呢份係L2，HR嘅budget最多得十一萬。
跟住個hiring manager好直接噉問我：「你要幾多先肯簽？」
我話：「如果有十一萬五，我即刻簽。」
結果我由十萬傾到十一萬五，bonus都係八個percent，冇變。
