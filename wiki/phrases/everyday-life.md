# Everyday Life

Answering 「最近點呀?」 when the honest answer is "busy and tired". That means the pets,
the day at the office, and everything still waiting at home afterwards. This is the
casual end of the wiki, with friends, family and anyone who asks how you've been.

Most of the useful words here are colloquial words that have a stiff written twin:
攰 vs 疲倦, 揀飲擇食 vs 偏食, 執屋 vs 打掃. words.hk labels most of those twins
書面語, which is the trap. **Complaining about being tired is where textbook Cantonese
sounds most like a textbook.**

The job itself is [[describing-my-job]]. Office talk is [[work-small-talk]].

## The pets

### 養咗兩隻貓 — I've got two cats
- **Jyutping:** joeng5 zo2 loeng5 zek3 maau1
- **Moment:** telling someone about the two cats I look after, and the new dog
- **Use:** 養 is "keep / raise" an animal, which is the ordinary way to say you *have* a
  pet. 隻 (zek3) is the measure word for animals, cats and dogs alike. For "recently got
  a dog as well": 最近多咗隻狗 (zeoi3 gan6 do1 zo2 zek3 gau2), literally "recently there's
  one more dog". The 一 drops, as it usually does before a measure word.
- **Register:** casual, universal.
- **Contrast:** 照顧 (ziu3 gu3) is "look after", which is correct but describes the
  caretaking, as with a sick relative or a friend's pet. For your own animals, 養 is the
  word. 有兩隻貓 also works, but 養 says they're yours to feed.
- **Example:** 我屋企養咗兩隻貓，最近仲多咗隻狗。— ngo5 uk1 kei2 joeng5 zo2 loeng5 zek3
  maau1, zeoi3 gan6 zung6 do1 zo2 zek3 gau2. "I've got two cats at home, and recently a dog
  as well." ⚠️ The sentence is composed. 養, 隻 and 多咗 are each verified.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[everyday-life#揀飲擇食]]

### 揀飲擇食 — a fussy eater
- **Jyutping:** gaan2 jam2 zaak6 sik6
- **Literal:** pick drinks, choose food
- **Moment:** the new dog is fussy and won't eat her kibble
- **Use:** The set phrase for picky eating. words.hk's examples are about children
  (細路仔…唔可以揀飲擇食㗎), so saying it about a dog carries a little humour: you're
  treating her like a spoiled kid.
- **Register:** casual, everyday.
- **Contrast:** 偏食 (pin1 sik6) means the same thing, but words.hk's example for it is
  「嚴重偏食會影響健康」, which is health-article register. 揀飲擇食 is what a person says;
  偏食 is what a nutritionist writes.
- **Example:** 隻狗好揀飲擇食，唔肯食狗糧。— zek3 gau2 hou2 gaan2 jam2 zaak6 sik6, m4 hang2
  sik6 gau2 loeng4. "The dog's really fussy, she won't eat her kibble."
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy
- **See also:** [[everyday-life#唔肯食]], [[final-particles#㗎 — gaa3 / gaa4]]

### 唔肯食 — won't eat it
- **Jyutping:** m4 hang2 sik6
- **Moment:** the dog refusing her kibble
- **Use:** 唔肯 + verb means unwilling, refusing. It works for anyone, including pets, kids
  and coworkers: 佢唔肯嚟, 佢唔肯講.
- **Register:** casual, universal.
- **Contrast:** 唔食 (m4 sik6) is "doesn't eat", a plain fact. 唔肯食 is "won't eat", a
  refusal, which is the point of the story. It's the same kind of split as 唔睇 vs 睇唔到
  in [[talking-about-money]].
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy

### 氹佢食 — coax her into eating
- **Jyutping:** tam3 keoi5 sik6
- **Moment:** we had to get wet food to coax her into eating the kibble
- **Use:** 氹 (tam3) is coax, cajole, sweet-talk someone into something. words.hk's example
  is 氹細路食藥, coaxing a kid into taking medicine, so it's exactly the right verb for a
  dog and her dinner.
- **Register:** casual, warm, a bit affectionate.
- **Contrast:** 哄 is the written character for the same idea; words.hk labels it 書面語.
  Note that 氹 also has other readings (dam4/tam5, "puddle"), but coaxing is tam3.
- **Example:** 唯有買罐頭返嚟氹佢食囉。— wai4 jau5 maai5 gun3 tau2 faan1 lai4 tam3 keoi5
  sik6 lo3. "So we had no choice but to buy wet food to coax her into eating." 唯有 is
  "the only option was", and 囉 (lo3) adds the shrug: what else could we do.
  ⚠️ The sentence is composed; 唯有, 罐頭, 氹 and 囉 lo3 are each verified.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[final-particles#囉 — lo1 / lo3]]

### 撈啲罐頭落去 — mix some wet food in
- **Jyutping:** lou1 di1 gun3 tau2 lok6 heoi3
- **Moment:** mixing wet food into the kibble so she'll eat it
- **Use:** 撈 (lou1) is to mix and stir, the same 撈 as 撈飯 (rice mixed with sauce).
  The pattern 撈 X 落 Y 度 is "mix X into Y".
- **Register:** casual, kitchen word.
- **Contrast:** 混合 and 攪拌 are mixing in a recipe or a lab. 撈 is what you do with a
  spoon over a bowl. On the words: 罐頭 (gun3 tau2, "a can") is what you'd say; 濕糧
  (wet food) and 乾糧 (dry food) are the pet-shop and packaging words. Royal Canin's
  Hong Kong site uses 濕糧, so it's real, just more "label" than "kitchen". 狗糧 on its own
  usually means the dry kibble.
- **Example:** 要撈啲罐頭落啲狗糧度，佢先肯食。— jiu3 lou1 di1 gun3 tau2 lok6 di1 gau2 loeng4
  dou6, keoi5 sin1 hang2 sik6. "We have to mix some wet food into the kibble, only then
  will she eat it." 先 here is "only then", the same 先 as 到期先攞得出嚟.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy · ⚠️ unverified
  (撈 lou1 "mix" is verified; the full sentence is composed)
- **See also:** [[talking-about-money#擺咗做定期]] (the same 先)

## The workday

### 返八個鐘工 — work eight hours
- **Jyutping:** faan1 baat3 go3 zung1 gung1
- **Moment:** I'm in the office for 8 hours as a software engineer
- **Use:** The duration goes *inside* 返工: 返 + hours + 工. words.hk's own example is
  「我一連返咗十五個鐘頭工」. 個鐘 and 個鐘頭 both mean "hour"; 個鐘 is shorter.
- **Register:** casual, universal.
- **Contrast:** 工作八小時 is written Chinese. 小時 is the word on a timetable, and 工作 is
  a verb nobody says out loud. Putting the hours after the whole verb (返工八個鐘) is
  the English word order and sounds translated. ⚠️ That last point is my judgment; the
  split-verb version is the one words.hk shows.
- **Example:** 我一日返八個鐘工。— ngo5 jat1 jat6 faan1 baat3 go3 zung1 gung1. "I work
  eight hours a day."
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy
- **See also:** [[work-small-talk#收工 — knock off]], [[describing-my-job]]

### 好燒腦 — really taxing on the brain
- **Jyutping:** hou2 siu1 nou5
- **Moment:** the job leaves my brain fried
- **Use:** 燒腦, literally "burns the brain", describes work that takes a lot of thinking.
  words.hk labels it 潮語 (trendy slang): current, not dated. It describes the *cause*,
  as in 「寫code好燒腦」. For the *state* afterwards, use 攰 (next block).
- **Register:** casual, young-ish, very natural for tech work.
- **Contrast:** 腦 is nou5 in the dictionary, but plenty of people say lou5. That's the
  n- → l- merger in [[lazy-sounds]]. 傷腦筋 is the older, more neutral "a headache to
  deal with".
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy
- **See also:** [[lazy-sounds]], [[software-and-tech]]

### 攰到散晒 — completely wiped out
- **Jyutping:** gui6 dou3 saan2 saai3
- **Moment:** after eight hours of code my brain is fried, and I still have to work out
- **Use:** 攰 (gui6) is *the* spoken word for tired. 攰到 X is "so tired that X", and
  散晒, literally "fallen apart completely" (俚語), is what words.hk shows after it:
  「個人攰到散晒」. Two more from the same shelf, both verified:
  - 謝晒皮 (ze6 saai3 pei4): "wiped out, totally spent". words.hk's example is about
    working fifteen hours straight, which makes it a work word.
  - 死死下 (sei2 sei2 haa2): "knackered, half-dead", often seen from outside:
    「你做咩死死下咁?」
  And for the recovery: 回血 (wui4 hyut3), "get my HP back", gaming slang that words.hk
  lists as plain "recover from tiredness".
- **Register:** casual. 散晒, 謝晒皮 and 死死下 are all slang, fine with friends.
- **Contrast:** this is the most important contrast on the page. 疲倦, 疲累 and 累 are all
  labelled **書面語** by words.hk, and 心力交瘁 and 精疲力竭 are four-character idioms that
  sound like a news report. Saying 我好疲倦 to a friend is the tiredness version of 完成
  for 搞掂.
- **Example:** 放工嗰陣，個人已經攰到散晒。— fong3 gung1 go2 zan6, go3 jan4 ji5 ging1 gui6
  dou3 saan2 saai3. "By the time I finish work, I'm completely wiped out."
  ⚠️ The sentence is composed; 放工 and 攰到散晒 are each verified.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[work-small-talk#做OT — work overtime]]

## Everything after work

### 放工仲要… — after work I still have to …
- **Jyutping:** fong3 gung1 zung6 jiu3 …
- **Moment:** after the office I still need to work out, cook and clean
- **Use:** 仲要 is "still have to, on top of that". It's the frame for listing what's left
  in the day, and the list is the complaint: 「放工仲要做gym、煮飯、執屋。」
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** 放工 (fong3 gung1) and 收工 (sau1 gung1) both mean finishing work.
  放工 is the employee's word ("I get off at six"); 收工 is more "wrap up, call it a day".
  下班 is the Mandarin form.
- **Example:** 放工仲要做gym、煮飯、執屋，乜都要自己搞。— fong3 gung1 zung6 jiu3 zou6 gym,
  zyu2 faan6, zap1 uk1, mat1 dou1 jiu3 zi6 gei2 gaau2. "After work I still have to go to
  the gym, cook, clean the flat. I have to handle everything myself."
  ⚠️ The sentence is composed from verified pieces.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[work-small-talk#收工 — knock off]]

### 做gym — work out
- **Jyutping:** zou6 gym
- **Moment:** needing to work out even though I'm tired
- **Use:** Going to the gym or working out. words.hk has it as a headword, also written
  做尖 (zou6 zim1), as in 「我去做尖，你一唔一齊?」.
- **Register:** casual, everywhere.
- **Contrast:** 做運動 (zou6 wan6 dung6) is "do exercise" generally: running, hiking, a
  doctor's advice. 健身 (gin6 san1) is the word on the gym's sign. Same English-wins slot
  as 寫code.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy
- **See also:** [[describing-my-job#寫code — write code]]

### 執屋 — tidy and clean the flat
- **Jyutping:** zap1 uk1
- **Moment:** the cleaning on top of everything else
- **Use:** 執 is "tidy, sort out", and 執屋 is the whole household job. Alongside it:
  煮飯 (zyu2 faan6, cook, literally "cook rice", meaning make a meal), 洗衫 (sai2 saam1,
  do laundry), 洗碗 (sai2 wun2, wash the dishes).
- **Register:** casual, universal.
- **Contrast:** 打掃 (daa2 sou3) is sweeping and scrubbing, and sounds a bit formal for
  "I clean up at home". 執屋 is what you'd say. 煮飯 means making any meal, not just rice;
  saying 做飯 is the Mandarin calque.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy · ⚠️ unverified
  (洗碗 isn't in the local tables; I'm confident it's sai2 wun2, but didn't check it)

### 時間唔夠用 — there just isn't enough time
- **Jyutping:** si4 gaan3 m4 gau3 jung6
- **Moment:** how hard it is to balance work and life
- **Use:** The summing-up line. 唔夠用 is "not enough to go round"; it also works for
  money (啲錢唔夠用). For the balance itself, the English is part of HK speech:
  「完全冇work-life balance」.
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** 平衡工作同生活 is the written way to put it, the sort of thing in a
  magazine article. Spoken HK reaches for the English phrase here, the same way it does
  with 做OT.
- **Example:** 真係好難平衡，時間永遠唔夠用。— zan1 hai6 hou2 naan4 ping4 hang4, si4 gaan3
  wing5 jyun5 m4 gau3 jung6. "It's really hard to balance; there's never enough time."
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-17 · practised 2026-11-17 · catching up, explaining why life is busy · ⚠️ unverified
  (every word is verified; whether 冇work-life balance is the commoner way to say it is
  my judgment, not a dictionary fact)
