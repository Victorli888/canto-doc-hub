# Filming My Family

Vlogging as a diary, not a business. This page covers explaining to friends why you're
filming at all, talking your parents into being filmed, and directing them once the
camera is rolling. The point is to keep their personalities for grandchildren who
haven't been born yet.

The vocabulary splits cleanly. The *gear* talk is half English (vlog, YouTuber), and
words.hk lists both. The *memory* talk is plain Cantonese: 記錄低, 睇返, 留返畀.
**The camera-directing lines, and the brush-offs, are where textbook Cantonese gives itself away.** 請你自然一點
is a film-school instruction, and 照平時咁講就得 is what a son says to his mom.

Talking to Mom about money is [[talking-about-money]]. What you're both busy with day to day is
[[everyday-life]].

## Why I'm doing it

### 拍片 / 拍vlog — film videos / make a vlog
- **Jyutping:** paak3 pin2 / paak3 vlog
- **Moment:** telling people I'm getting into videography and vlogging
- **Use:** 拍 is the verb for filming: 拍片, 拍戲, 拍vlog. 拍片 is the general "shoot
  video". *vlog* is said in English and used as an ordinary noun. HK retailers' buying
  guides say 「拍Vlog」 (YOHO, Fortress). 錄影 (luk6 jing2) is "record video" in the
  technical sense, as in CCTV.
- **Register:** casual, universal.
- **Contrast:** 攝影 (sip3 jing2) is photography as a craft or hobby label. 我最近學攝影
  is fine for stills, but for video in conversation it's 拍片. 影片 (jing2 pin2) is the
  noun "a video", and people mostly just say 條片 (tiu4 pin2) for one clip ⚠️ (條 as the
  measure word for a clip is not verified locally).
- **Example:** 我最近想學拍片，拍吓vlog。"I've been wanting to learn videography, do some
  vlogging." ⚠️ The sentence is composed.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · explaining my new hobby · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[filming-my-family#剪片]]

### 玩吓啫 — just for fun, no big deal
- **Jyutping:** waan2 haa5 ze1
- **Moment:** heading off the "so you want to be a YouTuber?" assumption
- **Use:** The full answer is 「唔係呀，玩吓啫。」. 玩 + 吓 (the "a bit, casually" suffix,
  as in 傾吓) + **啫** (ze1). words.hk glosses 啫 as 「只不過係噉」、「冇乜嘢」: "just that,
  no big deal". The line shrinks the claim instead of explaining it, which is how you
  brush off a friend's question. If you want to give the real reason, add it as a second
  sentence: 純粹想記錄低自己嘅生活.
- **Register:** casual, with friends and family.
- **Contrast:** The wiki first composed 「唔係呀，唔係為咗賺錢」 on 2026-11-20. That's
  grammatical, but 為咗 states a purpose, which sounds deliberate and explanatory for a
  throwaway question. The feedback's other options, from most to least casual:
  - 純粹係興趣啫 (seon4 seoi5 hai6 hing3 ceoi3 ze1): "it's purely a hobby"
  - 我又唔係想賺錢: "it's not like I'm trying to make money"
  - 唔係諗住賺錢㗎 (nam2 zyu6): "I'm not doing it with the intention of making money"
  - 我又唔係靠呢樣嘢搵食 (kaau3 … wan2 sik6): "it's not like I make a living from this"
  - 冇諗住靠呢樣嘢賺錢: "I'm not planning to make money from this"

  Four of the six play the claim down rather than state a purpose.
- **Example:** 「你想做YouTuber呀?」「唔係呀，玩吓啫。純粹想記錄低自己嘅生活。」"You
  want to be a YouTuber?" "Nah, it's just for fun. I just want to record my life." The
  first half comes from the feedback. The second half is composed, from verified pieces.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · explaining my new hobby · source: [[not-for-money-phrasing-feedback]]
- **See also:** [[filming-my-family#記錄低]], [[final-particles#啫 — ze1]], [[work-small-talk#傾吓]]

### 記錄低 — record / document
- **Jyutping:** gei3 luk6 dai1
- **Moment:** saying the point is to document my life
- **Use:** 記錄 + 低 means "record and keep". The 低 is the "leave it down / keep it"
  ending, as in 寫低 and 錄低 (luk6 dai1, record audio or video). words.hk's example is
  「我哋要記錄低依個重要嘅時刻」. 錄低 is the more literal "get it on tape".
- **Register:** neutral. Fine everywhere.
- **Contrast:** 紀錄 (gei2 luk6) with the other character is the *noun* "a record", as in
  a world record. 紀念 (gei2 nim6) is "commemorate", and 留念 (lau4 nim6) is "keep as a
  memento".
- **Example:** 我想將平時啲生活記錄低。"I want to record everyday life." ⚠️ The
  sentence is composed.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · explaining my new hobby · ⚠️ unverified

### 第日睇返 — watch it back someday
- **Jyutping:** dai6 jat6 tai2 faan1
- **Moment:** filming so that my future self can see it
- **Use:** 第日 (dai6 jat6) means "some day later". 睇返 (tai2 faan1) means "look back
  at, re-watch". words.hk uses it for re-reading minutes and account records. For "my
  future self", the natural phrasing is 將來嘅自己 (zoeng1 loi4 ge3 zi6 gei2), with 畀
  for "for … to watch": 畀將來嘅自己睇.
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** 日後 and 未來 sound like a speech. 第日 is what you say to your mom.
- **Example:** 第日老咗，可以睇返而家嘅自己。"Some day when I'm old I can look back at who
  I am now." ⚠️ The sentence is composed.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · explaining my new hobby · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[filming-my-family#留返畀…睇]]

## Filming my parents

### 留返畀…睇 — keep it for … to watch
- **Jyutping:** lau4 faan1 bei2 … tai2
- **Moment:** telling my parents I want to film them for their future grandchildren
- **Use:** 留返 (lau4 faan1) means "leave something for someone, set it aside". words.hk
  has 「其餘嘅留返俾你哋」. Put the viewer after 畀 and end with 睇: 留返畀啲孫睇. It's
  the core of the whole pitch to your parents.
- **Register:** casual, warm.
- **Contrast:** 留低 (lau4 dai1) is "leave behind", which is fine but has no recipient
  built in. 留返畀 says *who* it's for, and that's the emotional point.
- **Example:** 我想拍吓你哋，留返畀第日啲孫睇。"I want to film you two, to keep for the
  grandkids someday." ⚠️ The sentence is composed. 留返, 畀, 第日 and 孫 are each verified.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · persuading my parents to be filmed · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[filming-my-family#啲孫]]

### 啲孫 — the grandkids
- **Jyutping:** di1 syun1
- **Moment:** the future grandchildren the videos are for
- **Use:** 孫 (syun1) is grandchild through a son. 孫仔 (syun1 zai2) is a grandson, and 孫女
  (syun1 neoi2) a granddaughter. 啲孫 means "the grandkids" generally. To your children,
  your parents will be **爺爺 (je4 je2)** and **嫲嫲 (maa4 maa4)**, the father's side.
  That's the framing to use with them: 等啲孫知道爺爺嫲嫲係點嘅人 ⚠️.
- **Register:** family, warm.
- **Contrast:** The child of a *daughter* is 外孫 (ngoi6 syun1), and those grandparents
  are 公公 (gung4 gung1) / 婆婆 (po4 po2). Cantonese marks which side of the family.
  Get it backwards and your parents will notice.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · persuading my parents to be filmed
- **See also:** [[filming-my-family#性格 / 口頭禪]]

### 性格 / 口頭禪 — personality / catchphrase
- **Jyutping:** sing3 gaak3 / hau2 tau4 sim4
- **Moment:** wanting to capture my parents' personalities on film
- **Use:** 性格 is "personality, character". 口頭禪 (hau2 tau4 sim4) is the thing they
  always say, and it's the most filmable bit of anyone's personality. Words for the
  personalities themselves, all verified:
  - 搞笑 (gaau2 siu3): funny
  - 得意 (dak1 ji3): cute, endearing
  - 鬼馬 (gwai2 maa5): playful, witty
  - 開心果 (hoi1 sam1 gwo2): the one who cheers everyone up
  - 長氣 (coeng4 hei3): goes on and on
  - 脾氣 (pei4 hei3): temper
- **Register:** casual. Teasing a parent as 長氣 is affectionate if you're smiling.
- **Contrast:** 個性 also means personality, but "show off their personality" in English
  doesn't translate word for word. Say what you want people to *see*: 等啲孫睇吓爺爺有幾搞笑
  ("so the grandkids can see how funny Grandpa is") ⚠️.
- **Example:** 你個口頭禪一定要拍落嚟！"Your catchphrase has to be on film!" ⚠️ The
  sentence is composed. 拍落嚟 follows the same pattern as 記錄落嚟, but is unverified
  as a set phrase.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · persuading my parents to be filmed · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[everyday-life]]

## Behind the camera

### 照平時咁就得 — just be like you normally are
- **Jyutping:** ziu3 ping4 si4 gam2 zau6 dak1
- **Moment:** my parents stiffening up the moment the camera's on
- **Use:** 照 means "following, as per". 平時 means "normally". 咁就得 means "that's all
  it takes". The whole line means "just act normal". It pairs with 自然啲 (zi6 jin4 di1,
  "a bit more natural"). words.hk defines 自然 as 輕鬆；唔勉強、唔拘謹.
- **Register:** casual, family.
- **Contrast:** 請你自然一點 is Mandarin-shaped and sounds like a photographer at a
  studio. 一點 is the giveaway, since Cantonese says 啲. 唔使理部機 ("don't mind the
  camera") is another common way in ⚠️. 部 is verified as the measure word for a camera
  (相機, 量詞：部), but the phrase is not.
- **Example:** 唔使理部機，照平時咁傾偈就得。"Don't mind the camera, just chat like you
  normally do." ⚠️ The sentence is composed.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · filming my parents · ⚠️ unverified
- **See also:** [[talking-about-money#畀你平時用嘅]] (平時 over 日常, again)

### 望住個鏡頭 — look at the camera
- **Jyutping:** mong6 zyu6 go3 geng3 tau4
- **Moment:** directing my parents during a shot
- **Use:** The phrase is verified word for word: words.hk's example for 鏡頭 is
  「望住個鏡頭。」 The opposite is 唔使望鏡頭 ("no need to look at the camera"), which is
  what you'll say more often for a natural vlog.
- **Register:** neutral.
- **Contrast:** 鏡頭 (geng3 tau4) is the lens, or a single shot. 相機 (soeng2 gei1) is the
  camera itself, measure word 部. Your phone is 手機.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · filming my parents
- **See also:** [[filming-my-family#唔鍾意上鏡]]

### 再嚟過 — let's do that again
- **Jyutping:** zoi3 lai4 gwo3
- **Moment:** asking for another take
- **Use:** 再 + verb + 過 means "do it over from scratch". words.hk has 「轉頭再嚟過!」 and
  「聽日再嚟過啦」. 再嚟多次 (zoi3 lai4 do1 ci3) means "one more time", also attested.
  Add 啦 to make it a gentle suggestion: 再嚟過啦.
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** 重新拍一次 is understandable, but stiff and spelled out. 再嚟過 is what
  people actually say on set and off it.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · filming my parents
- **See also:** [[final-particles]]

### 剪片 — edit video
- **Jyutping:** zin2 pin2
- **Moment:** explaining what I do with the footage afterwards
- **Use:** It literally means "cut the film". words.hk covers trimming and reordering.
  It's the everyday word for editing. 剪好 means "finished editing": 剪好畀你睇 ⚠️.
- **Register:** casual, universal.
- **Contrast:** 編輯 (pin1 cap1) is "edit", as for text, and sounds like a job title.
  For video it's 剪.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · explaining my new hobby
- **See also:** [[filming-my-family#拍片 / 拍vlog]]

## Their side of the conversation

### 唔鍾意上鏡 — don't like being on camera
- **Jyutping:** m4 zung1 ji3 soeng5 geng3
- **Moment:** a parent waving the camera away
- **Use:** 上鏡 (soeng5 geng3) means "photogenic, look good on camera". words.hk has the
  exact line you'll hear: 「我唔鍾意上鏡，你唔好影我啦。」 Other ways they'll say it:
  - 怕醜 (paa3 cau2): shy
  - 樣衰 (joeng2 seoi1): look awful, or feel embarrassed
  - 拍我做咩呀: what are you filming me for? ⚠️
- **Register:** casual.
- **Contrast:** Your comeback, verified word for word: **真人靚過上鏡** (zan1 jan4 leng3
  gwo3 soeng5 geng3), "you look better in person than on camera". Turn it round for a
  parent: 你上鏡好睇呀 ⚠️.
- **Status:** learning · wished 2026-11-20 · practised 2026-11-23 · persuading my parents to be filmed
- **See also:** [[filming-my-family#照平時咁就得]]
