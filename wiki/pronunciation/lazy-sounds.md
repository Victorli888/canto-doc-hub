# 懶音 — Lazy Sounds

懶音 (laan5 jam1), "lazy sounds": a set of sound mergers pervasive in casual Hong Kong
speech, especially among younger speakers. Newsreaders and formal speech avoid them;
almost everyone else uses at least some.

This page matters in both directions. **Comprehension:** if you're listening for 我 as
*ngo5*, you will miss it, because plenty of people say *o5*. **Production:** a learner who
pronounces every textbook initial crisply sounds like a newsreader at the dinner table —
hitting 我 as *ngo5* every single time is its own kind of tell.

The honest guidance: know the careful form, and let the merged form happen in casual
speech rather than drilling against it. Keep the careful form for anything formal.

## n- → l-

Worked instance: 燒腦 (siu1 nou5) is often siu1 lou5 — see [[everyday-life#好燒腦]].

你 nei5 → **lei5** · 男 naam4 → **laam4** · 腦 nou5 → **lou5**

The most widespread merger by far, and the most harmless — *lei5* for 你 is
unremarkable from almost any speaker in almost any register.

## Dropped ng-

我 ngo5 → **o5** · 牛 ngau4 → **au4** · 銀 ngan4 → **an4**

Common and casual. Noticeably informal.

## Added ng- (hypercorrection)

愛 oi3 → **ngoi3** · 鴨 aap3 → **ngaap3**

The mirror image: an ng- appearing on syllables that never had one, from speakers
over-applying the correction. Worth recognizing, not worth imitating.

## gw- → g-

國 gwok3 → **gok3** · 廣 gwong2 → **gong2**

So 廣東話 gwong2 dung1 waa2 becomes *gong2 dung1 waa2* — including when people say the
name of the language they're speaking.

## Final -ng → -n

香港 hoeng1 gong2 → hoeng1 **gon2** · 恆生 hang4 saang1 → **han4 saan1**

The merger that costs the most comprehension, because it collapses pairs that were
distinct. Expect it; produce the careful form.

## Syllabic 五

五 ng5 → **m5**

Very common when counting or giving a price.
