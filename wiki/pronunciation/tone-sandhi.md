# Changed Tone (變調)

Some syllables are pronounced on a *higher* tone than the character's dictionary reading
when they sit in a particular compound. This is 變調, changed tone, and it is not
optional or regional — using the dictionary tone is one of the clearest signs of having
read a word rather than heard it. Written here as `gin6-2`: dictionary tone 6, surfaces
as tone 2.

Distinct from [[lazy-sounds]], which is about consonants being relaxed. Changed tone is
not laziness; it is the correct form, and getting it wrong is a mistake rather than a
register choice.

## The 件 rule

件 is gin6 on its own and in 一件事 (jat1 gin6 si6). In the tech compounds 軟件, 硬件 and
組件 it is **gin2**. Three words, one rule, and all three are words Victor needs at work
— so this one is worth drilling rather than looking up.

## What this page is not yet

A general account of when 變調 applies. The honest position: the triggering conditions
are partly lexical, so the reliable method is to learn it per word rather than derive it.
Entries get `gin6-2` notation when the precision is worth it, and this page collects
them as they accumulate.

One thing checked and worth recording as a negative: **雪櫃 is syut3 gwai6, not
gwai6-2.** The pattern does not generalise to every noun compound, so guessing a raised
tone is as wrong as missing one.
