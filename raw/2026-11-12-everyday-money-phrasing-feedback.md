It's understandable, but it sounds more like something translated into Cantonese rather than how a Hong Kong native would spontaneously say it.
What does 「係你日常用嗰筆」mean?
Breaking it down:
- 係 — is
- 你日常用 — what you use on a daily basis
- 嗰筆 — that sum of money
Together: "That's the money you use for everyday expenses."

The unusual part isn't 「嗰筆」 itself. Hong Kong people absolutely say 「嗰筆錢」. It's the combination of 「日常用」 and 「嗰筆」 that makes it sound somewhat stiff.
How I'd say it in natural HK Cantonese
Here are three alternatives:
Cantonese	English	Naturalness
嗰五千蚊係畀你平時用嘅。	That $5,000 is for your everyday use.	Very natural
仲有五千蚊喺 checking account，畀你平時使嘅。	Another $5,000 is in checking, for you to spend.	Very conversational
仲有五千蚊擺喺 checking account，方便你平時用。	Another $5,000 is in checking so you can use it whenever.	Natural and explanatory

「平時用」 is much more conversational than 「日常用」 in this context.
My preferred version of your entire sentence
Wells Fargo 嗰邊，有兩萬蚊擺咗做定期，未到期就攞唔到出嚟。另外仲有五千蚊喺 checking account，畀你平時用嘅。

This sounds more like how someone in Hong Kong would explain the situation to a family member.

A few other differences worth noticing:
- 「擺咗做定期」 sounds more conversational than 「擺咗落定期存款」.
- 「未到期就攞唔到出嚟」 is natural, although technically some fixed deposits allow early withdrawal with penalties.
- 「畀你平時用」 is more idiomatic than 「係你日常用嗰筆」.
One particularly useful distinction for your Cantonese learning:

「日常」 isn't wrong or non-Hong Kong Cantonese. Hong Kong people use it frequently in expressions like 「日常生活」 and 「日常開支」. But when talking casually about money you use every day, 「平時使」 or 「平時用」 will generally sound more native.
